So nearly all celebrates will have approximately seven pages. A home page, an about page, a processes page, a services page, an FAQ page, internal links page, a contact page. All celebrate websites must have the [APC certified by logo](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1Kw6PW_o3f6hF2t43VmCzltBqBqiAahpR?usp=drive_link) on the website somewhere. And it is important and favoured to try and mention them where you can and always link their mentions to the [https://www.funeralcelebrantacademy.co.uk](https://www.funeralcelebrantacademy.co.uk). The footer should say at the bottom, somewhere, website made by the [Academy of Professional Celebrants](https://www.funeralcelebrantacademy.co.uk). 

No website should ever have a picture of a priest. 

No website should use em-dashes unless a celebrate has specifically included an em-dash in their copy

All spelling should be British English spelling as opposed to American spelling. So essentially this just means look out for the Z’s\!

My key recommendations for the design are to use backgrounds a lot for each section and to use animation tools like frame emotion, GSAP, and possibly Leninus brackets, although I would say that maybe to never turn on the Leninus scrolling function control and keep to the browsers scrolling because many celebrities will assume this is just website lag.

In terms of the design, apart from adding backgrounds and animation, other suggestions I would have is to favor broken grids, background videos in the hero, fancy entrance animations, touches of the suggested branding that they have. For example, dotting around faded icons in random places. 

In terms of images, everything needs to be unlicensed. So my main recommendations are:  
Either generating them using AI. If you would like, you can [use my tool](https://batch-image-creator.pages.dev) or get images from [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com). I've had complaints about generating some images that don't have them in it, but then maybe a generated image of a celebrate. People don't like that, so avoid that. Usually images generated probably would just favour like more abstract landscapes and stuff like that, to negate the risk of them complaining about the fact that there's people in it and they don't want there to be thats not them.

All images are often provided by the celebrates in an unusable manner because they're screenshots from an iPhone or giant JPEG files. I do what I can to compress the images enough or replicate them but often it does need to be the case that really I just generate the images myself or find them online

All celebrants definitely favor ‘louder’ designs at the expense of accessibility. So yes, focus very much on ways in which you can make it have a lot of ‘personality’, even if that disregards many common design rules that we often see when we are developing a site. Of course make sure that be the text, the backgrounds, but I wouldn't worry about attempting to get to a AAA WGAC accessibility standard at the expense of it not feeling like a generic design.

If you are using AI to help develop the website, some of the most helpful tools that I have found are using claude’s [front-end design skills](https://skills.sh/anthropics/skills/frontend-design) and the [awwwards skill for animations](https://skills.sh/devmartinese/awwwards-animations-skill/awwwards-animations).

One of my main recommendations is because of the lack of context window for many tools and of course I don't know how your context is managed but for me because I'm quite lazy about it I often find that I haven't ever given tool enough information in order to make sure it sticks to the key guidelines and the document provided.

So I would always get your tool to do a final check before you deploy the final deployment. Just making sure that we're doing everything they want and if we decided to not follow things exactly, we have a bit of a reason why. One example that I could give of this is that for instance often many celebrates choose to make their home page to include all of the information about them in the homepage. But this would lead to then repeating ourselves again on the about page. So I often ignore the fact that they want all of their information on the home page for the sake of not getting another complaint about the fact that I have put duplicate content in particular places.
